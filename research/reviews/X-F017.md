# Review of X-F017 — Repeated parsed balance-sheet signatures

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and sharpened from "needs review" to DATA ARTIFACT.**
Codex identified the pattern and correctly declined to interpret it. I ran the
diagnostic that resolves it, and the answer is that most of these are
extraction reuse, not aliasing.

## Independent reproduction

| Statistic | Codex | Claude | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| preferred rows with all three core balance variables | 863 | 863 | exact |
| repeated signatures | 51 | 51 | exact |
| rows involved | 104 (12.05%) | 104 (12.05%) | exact |

Signature = `fiscal_year` × `currency` × `units` × `total_assets` ×
`total_liabilities` × `equity`.

## The diagnostic Codex proposed and did not run

Codex listed four candidate explanations — alias, shared venture, legitimate
equal balance, extraction error — and said they should be separated before
econometric use. Splitting the 51 clusters by source and by entity name does
exactly that:

| Property of the cluster | clusters |
|---|---|
| all rows share one `source_url` | **49 of 51** |
| rows carry **different** entity names | 35 of 51 |
| both: one source URL, different entity names | **33 of 51** |
| span more than one parent group | 20 of 51 |
| equity exactly zero | 5 of 51 |
| total assets exactly zero | 0 of 51 |

Two worked examples make the mechanism obvious:

```
TATA COMMUNICATIONS TRANSFORMATION SERVICES HUNGARY KFT   INR  TA 7006.635  EQ 8401.773
TATA COMMUNICATIONS TRANSFORMATION SERVICES PTE LTD       INR  TA 7006.635  EQ 8401.773
TATA COMMUNICATIONS TRANSFORMATION SERVICES USA INC       INR  TA 7006.635  EQ 8401.773
                                     (all three: www.tatacommunications.com, same URL)

AUTOMOBILI PININFARINA AMERICAS INC   USD  TA 542964  EQ 11131
PININFARINA OF AMERICA CORP           USD  TA 542964  EQ 11131
                                     (both: www.mahindra.com, same URL)
```

These are not aliases and not shared ventures. They are three legally distinct
Tata Communications subsidiaries — Hungarian, Singaporean and American — that
have all been assigned the same numbers out of one combined-PDF section,
because their names share a long prefix. The "legitimate equal balance" and
"shell with zero equity" explanations are ruled out: no cluster has zero
assets, and only 5 have zero equity.

## Severity for the parent project

The contamination lands squarely inside the valuation sample.

- 70 of the 104 duplicate-signature rows are flagged `ready_for_valuation == 1`
  — **12.5% of all 560 ready rows**.
- Of the 35 ready-row signature clusters, 24 contain more than one entity name,
  covering **49 ready rows**.

So roughly one ready row in nine carries a balance sheet that at least one
other entity also claims, and in most of those cases the entities are
demonstrably different companies. Any OFBV net-worth aggregate built on the
560 ready rows will double- or triple-count those balance sheets.

## Tests I added beyond the reproduction

- **Unique-entity versus row counting.** Collapsing to one row per target does
  not fix it: the duplicates are across targets, not within them.
- **Cross-parent check.** 20 of 51 clusters span two parent groups. Those are
  the ones where a genuine shared venture is plausible (the consortium
  vehicles in my C-F012), so they should be separated from the 33
  same-URL/different-name clusters before any manual audit.
- **Missing-data direction.** This is a false-positive problem, not a
  missingness problem: it inflates apparent coverage while degrading accuracy,
  which is the more dangerous direction for a valuation exercise.

## The evidence columns settle it

Codex's proposed test was to compare the `*_evidence` source text across rows
in a cluster: if the text differs, identical values would be coincidence; if it
is the same, one parsed line has been assigned to several entities. I ran it.

**All 33 same-URL / different-name clusters have byte-identical
`equity_evidence` and byte-identical `total_assets_evidence` across every row
in the cluster.** One extracted line is being attributed to two or three
distinct legal entities.

The evidence text for the Tata Communications cluster also shows *why*:

```
Total Equity .84.01,773)|(1,72,99.69.088)(2,24.01,683)| (1,90,51.18,1 70)
TOTAL EQUITY AND LIABILITIES cts pe [ ~_70.06,635 | 65.87.0870 | $2,63.564 | 70,64,52.088
```

This is a garbled OCR capture of a **multi-column** table — one row of a
combined statement covering four subsidiaries side by side — from which the
parser took a single number. The duplication is therefore not merely section
reuse; it is a column-alignment failure in a combined-subsidiary PDF. Values
parsed from such lines are unreliable even for the one entity they were
assigned to.

This moves the finding from "requires source-level review" to a confirmed
extraction artefact with a diagnosable cause, and gives the parent project a
concrete, mechanical filter: drop or re-parse any row whose `*_evidence` text
is shared with another target.
