# Review of X-F010 — Zero mapping stakes are not literal zero ownership

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** This is the cleanest falsification on either branch:
it is not an argument about plausibility, it is a direct contradiction inside
the file.

## Independent reproduction

| Statistic | Codex | Claude | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| level-0 targets with missing stake | 184/184 | 184/184 | exact |
| non-root targets with stake exactly zero | 406/1,650 = 24.61% | 406/1,650 = 24.61% | exact |
| preferred rows carrying both `stake` and `shareholding_percent` | 45 | 45 | exact |
| rows with `stake == 0` but positive AOC shareholding | 7 | 7 | exact |
| rows where the two differ by more than 1pp | 8 | 8 | exact |

The seven contradicting AOC values are 73.94, and 100.00 six times. Six of
seven entities that the mapping records as a zero stake are recorded in the
parent's own AOC-1 table as **wholly owned**.

## Why this is decisive

The two measures come from different upstream sources — `stake` from the
subsidiary mapping, `shareholding_percent` from the parsed AOC-1 table — so
the comparison is a genuine external check rather than an internal
consistency test. Where both exist, `stake == 0` is wrong 7 times out of the
7 zero cases available.

## Test I added

I checked whether the zeros behave like a real economic quantity or like a
missing code. In increment 1 I found the zero share falls monotonically with
depth: 33.5% at level 1, 17.5% at levels 2–3, 10.1% at level 6, and **0% at
every level from 7 to 12**. A real ownership share has no reason to become
systematically non-zero as you descend a chain; a coverage gap in the mapping
does. Combined with Codex's AOC contradiction, the missing-code reading is now
well supported from two directions.

I also confirm the parent spread Codex reports: the zero share ranges from
0.78% (Airtel) to 100% (the Reliance Energy bucket), which is a
source-availability pattern, not an ownership pattern.

## Consequence for both branches

Any cumulative-control calculation that multiplies stakes down a chain will
return zero for a quarter of all edges. Codex's instruction — do not estimate
control until this is repaired — is correct. My own C-F015 (control-weighted
denominators) uses a 10% stake floor, which **deletes these 406 real
subsidiaries along with the genuine minority holdings**. That is a flaw in my
filter, not in Codex's finding, and I have corrected it below.

**Corrected rule, adopted for both:** treat `stake == 0` as missing, and apply
a minority filter only to entities with a strictly positive recorded stake.
Under that rule the minority (<10%) set falls from 481 entities to **75**, of
which 42 are Reliance (38 of them Breakthrough Energy Ventures portfolio
companies) and 20 are Wipro; the remaining 13 are spread across six groups.

## Mundane explanation

Numeric zero used where missing would be semantically correct, most likely
because the upstream mapping stored a numeric column with no null
representation. Effective-date mismatches between the mapping and the financial
statement could explain differences of a few percentage points, but not a
0-versus-100 gap.
