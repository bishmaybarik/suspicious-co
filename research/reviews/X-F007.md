# Review of X-F007 — Netherlands and Mauritius are path intermediaries

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** The claim reproduces closely, and — importantly — it
survives the falsification test Codex proposed but did not run.

## The claim

The Netherlands is 6.01% of normalised entities but 19.69% of observed-entity
appearances in complete ownership paths; equal-parent weighting gives 7.97%
versus 14.61%. Mauritius is 3.11% versus 8.11% (equal-parent 3.65% versus
8.26%). Dutch parent nodes generate 299 non-root children, 75.59% cross-border,
across 75 destinations and 16 groups, with the largest group supplying 30.10%.

Residual differences are entirely the entity denominator: Codex collapses
three Dutch name variants that I keep separate (110 versus 113 Dutch entities),
and one child edge each for NL and MU sits on a path I treat as re-anchored.

## Definitions and denominators compared

Codex's ancestry share counts a jurisdiction **once for every descendant path
that traverses it**. Codex itself flags the mechanical concern: "shared
prefixes are repeated once for every descendant path, mechanically making
branching intermediaries prominent." That is exactly right, and it means the
headline 19.69% is not by itself evidence of a special Dutch role — a single
Dutch node with 100 descendants would produce it.

## Tests I added

**1. Unique-intermediary-node counting (removes all path repetition).**
Restricting to the 404 entities that hold at least one subsidiary:

| Jurisdiction | share of all entities | share of *unique* intermediary nodes | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Netherlands | 6.16% | **15.10%** | 2.45× |
| Mauritius | 3.11% | **6.19%** | 1.99× |
| United States | 19.79% | 17.08% | 0.86× |

The over-representation survives with every node counted exactly once. This is
the decisive test and Codex did not run it.

**2. Leaf-only ancestry (Codex's own suggested falsification).**
Counting ancestors of leaf entities only, excluding the leaf itself and the
Indian root:

| Jurisdiction | entity share | leaf-ancestor share | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jersey | 0.11% | 1.60% | 14.7× |
| Luxembourg | 0.49% | 2.40% | 4.9× |
| Netherlands | 6.16% | 24.95% | 4.1× |
| Cyprus | 0.82% | 3.24% | 4.0× |
| Mauritius | 3.11% | 10.19% | 3.3× |
| United Kingdom | 5.40% | 6.91% | 1.3× |
| **United States** | 19.79% | 17.36% | **0.88×** |

The US — the largest entity jurisdiction — is *under*-represented as an
ancestor. The pattern is therefore not a mechanical artefact of large entity
counts.

**3. Descendants per unique intermediary node, by jurisdiction.**
Netherlands: 61 intermediary nodes across 16 parents, mean 14.4 descendants.
Mauritius: 25 nodes across 10 parents, mean 13.3. United States: 69 nodes
across 16 parents, mean 8.0. Germany: 25 nodes, mean 6.3.

## Missing-data sensitivity

150 entities sit on a path containing an unobserved intermediary
(`path_has_unobserved == 1`). Those unobserved nodes carry no country label,
so they can only *understate* every jurisdiction's ancestry share. The largest
of them (`DR REDDY'S LABORATORIES SA`, 29 children) is Swiss-sounding rather
than Dutch or Mauritian, so filling it in would not manufacture this result.

## Mundane explanations

Regional holding companies for European operations (Netherlands), and
historical India–Mauritius investment routing, are standard corporate
structuring. Much of the Dutch mass is inherited: SMRP BV and Novelis came with
acquisitions rather than being built afterwards. Nothing here speaks to tax or
legal treatment.

## Relation to my own work

This overlaps my C-F002 (gateway amplification) and C-F006 (jurisdiction
roles) but is measured differently and reaches the same conclusion by a
different route: Codex measures *ancestry frequency along paths*, I measure
*descendants per first-hop entity*. Agreement across two independent
denominators strengthens both.
