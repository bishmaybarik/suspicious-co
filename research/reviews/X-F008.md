# Review of X-F008 — Most non-root ownership edges cross jurisdictions

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## Independent reproduction

| Statistic | Codex | Claude | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| non-root edges crossing jurisdictions | 951/1,650 = 57.64% | 948/1,650 = 57.45% | yes |
| equal-parent mean | 58.67% | 58.48% | yes |
| leave-one-parent-out range | 56.06% to 61.78% | 55.87% to 61.58% | yes |

The three-edge difference is a name-normalisation difference in how each of us
resolves a handful of alias parents; it moves the estimate by 0.19pp.

## Definitions compared

The critical choice is the exclusion of level-0 edges. Every level-0 edge runs
India → foreign by construction, so including them forces the cross-border rate
upward mechanically. My own increment-1 number was 64.1% *including* those
edges, which is the same data and a worse denominator. Codex's exclusion is
correct and I have adopted it.

A second definitional point: Codex uses the *recorded*
`immediate_parent_country` rather than the country of the reconstructed graph
parent. These differ for the 78 entities whose named parent is absent from the
target table. Using the recorded field is the right call here — it keeps those
entities in the sample with a real country label rather than dropping them or
imputing.

## Tests I added

**Unique-entity versus path-level counting.** Each of the 1,650 non-root
entities contributes exactly one edge, so this statistic is already
unique-entity counted. There is no path repetition to strip out. This is why
it is so stable.

**Hierarchy mechanics.** A concern would be that deep chains mechanically
cross borders more. They do not do so monotonically: the pooled rate by
reported level is non-monotonic (Codex reports level 3 at 55.0% and level 5 at
75.0% pooled, but 29.0% and 49.3% equal-parent). I confirm the non-monotonicity
and agree with Codex's conclusion that level-specific rates are parent
composition, not structure.

**Missing-data sensitivity.** The 406 zero-stake entities and the 150
unobserved-intermediary paths do not enter this statistic at all — it needs
only two country labels per entity, and both are never missing. That is the
main reason this is the most robust structural number either branch produced.

## Mundane explanation

Regional holding companies own operating companies in the markets they serve;
acquisitions preserve the acquired group's cross-country parents. A majority
cross-border rate is what a multinational with regional holdings should look
like. The finding's value is that it is *measured* and stable, giving a
baseline against which individual groups (Jindal 97% conduit exposure, Biocon
0%) can be read as deviations.

## Relation to my own work

Complements my C-F003 (conduit exposure). C-F003 asks whether a *specific*
class of jurisdiction sits upstream; X-F008 asks whether *any* border is
crossed. They are different questions with consistent answers, and X-F008 is
the more robust of the two because it has no jurisdiction list to argue about.
