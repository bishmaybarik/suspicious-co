# Review of X-F014 — US concentration is real but denominator- and parent-sensitive

**Verdict: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED.** Every headline number reproduces. The one
component that needs qualifying is the "India → US → US" leaf motif, which is
much larger under a slightly different and equally defensible path definition,
and correspondingly less Reliance-driven.

## Independent reproduction

| Statistic | Codex | Claude | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| US share of entities | 363/1,830 = 19.84% | 363/1,834 = 19.79% | yes |
| US raw-row share | 17.00% | 17.00% | exact |

| Reliance share of US level-1 entities | 52.66% | 52.66% | exact |
| leaf motif India → US → US | 158 paths, Reliance 61.39% | 158 paths, Reliance 61.39% | exact |

The last line reproduces exactly once I adopt Codex's definition: a path of
**exactly three nodes** (India, one US parent, one US leaf).

## The qualification

Under my own definition — collapse consecutive same-country hops, so
"India → US" covers any number of US layers — the same motif covers **213 leaf
entities, of which Reliance supplies 48.83%**, spread over the same ten
parents. The extra 55 entities are US chains three or more US layers deep
(Wipro and Sun Pharma contribute most of them), which Codex's three-node
definition excludes.

Neither definition is wrong. But the difference matters for the interpretation
Codex draws: "Reliance supplies 61.39%" reads as one group dominating a motif,
while "Reliance supplies 48.83% of the broader same-country US motif" reads as
one group being merely the largest of ten. The honest statement is that the
Reliance share of the US same-country layering motif is between 49% and 61%
depending on whether deeper US stacks are counted.

## Tests I added

**Control weighting.** Reliance's 97 entities in the three-node motif include
its Breakthrough Energy Ventures portfolio companies, which are not
subsidiaries. Removing entities whose immediate parent is one of the two BEV
vehicles cuts Reliance's contribution sharply and takes the US level-1 share
with it — this is the same denominator problem as my C-F015, arriving through
a different statistic.

**Unique-entity counting.** The US entity share is unaffected by path
repetition (each entity is counted once), so the 19.8% figure is solid. It is
the *motif* statistics, which are path-level, that move with definition.

**Leave-one-parent-out.** Codex reports 15.36%–22.09% for the US entity share;
consistent with my own recomputation. The US remains the largest entity
jurisdiction under every drop.

## Where I disagree with the emphasis, not the numbers

Codex ranks this 13th of 17, which I think is right. The more interesting fact
in the same data is the one visible in my review of X-F007: the US is the
largest entity jurisdiction but the *only* large jurisdiction that is
**under-represented as an ancestor** (17.4% of leaf-ancestor appearances
against 19.8% of entities, a ratio of 0.88 versus 4.1 for the Netherlands).
The US is where Indian groups put operating companies and funds; it is not
where they put the holding layer. That contrast is worth more than the level
of US concentration itself, and neither branch has stated it directly.

## Mundane explanation

The US is the largest destination market for Indian IT, pharmaceutical and
energy investment, and US-to-US layering reflects domestic sub-holding and
fund structures that have nothing to do with cross-border structuring.
