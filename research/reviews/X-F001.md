# Review of X-F001 — Raw rows radically misweight parent exposure

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## Independent reproduction

| Statistic | Codex | Claude | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tata Communications raw-row share | 14.70% (550 rows) | 14.70% (550 rows) | exact |
| Tata Communications entity share | 2.68% | 2.73% (50/1,834) | yes |
| Motherson raw-row share | 8.87% (332 rows) | 8.87% (332 rows) | exact |
| Motherson entity share | 16.89% | 16.85% (309/1,834) | yes |
| Tata Communications preferred rows per target | 11 | 11.0 | exact |

Entity-share differences are the 1,830-versus-1,834 denominator: Codex
collapses four alias pairs that I keep distinct.

## Why the finding is right and worth its rank

The row-share to entity-share ratio spans an order of magnitude across groups
(Tata Communications 5.4×, Motherson 0.53×). Any statistic computed on raw
rows is therefore substantially a statement about **how many years of
statements were located per entity**, which is a property of the scraper and
the parent's filing practice, not of corporate structure.

Tata Communications is the extreme case for a specific and verifiable reason:
it has 11 preferred rows per target, the longest panel in the file, and 92% of
its entities are valuation-ready at least once — by far the highest coverage of
any group. It is simultaneously the smallest big-group network (50 entities
across 32 jurisdictions) and the most heavily sampled.

finding for any user of this file. I have adopted it.
